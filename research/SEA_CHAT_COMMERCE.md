# Selling Mid-to-High-Ticket Wellness/Beauty over Chat: MY / SG / BN / HK

**Purpose:** tuning an AI sales assistant that currently pitches an RM188–RM1180 bundle, asks for the sale too fast, sends no images, and reads as untrustworthy.

**Date of research:** 2026-07-30

---

## HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT

Every substantive claim is tagged:

- **[CITED]** — traceable to a named source, with URL. A sub-tag indicates source quality:
  - `(regulator)` — government / statutory body. Highest reliability.
  - `(news/industry)` — mainstream press, trade press, consumer council.
  - `(practitioner)` — real sales-training material actually used by sellers in-market. Reliable as evidence of *what sellers do*, not as evidence that it works.
  - `(vendor)` — martech / SaaS blog. **Treat all numbers from these with suspicion.** They are usually unsourced, self-serving, and frequently recycled between blogs. I have flagged where a number looks fabricated.
- **[INFERENCE]** — my reasoning, not data.
- **[NO DATA FOUND]** — I looked and could not find it. Stated plainly rather than filled in.

**Overall honesty warning about the evidence base:** there is essentially **no peer-reviewed or first-party-platform research** on the specific questions asked (image-order in chat, exchanges-before-price, per-market pushiness tolerance). The genuinely high-quality material I found falls into two buckets: (1) **regulators**, which are excellent and very actionable, and (2) **local-language practitioner sales scripts**, which are excellent for reverse-engineering actual behaviour. The "conversion lift %" numbers circulating in vendor blogs are largely unverifiable and I have not built rules on them.

## 1. VISUAL PROOF — what buyers expect to SEE, and in what order

### 1a. What the actual practitioner scripts do

The single most useful artifact I found is a Malay-language WhatsApp closing script sold to Malaysian online sellers — a **13-step sequence**. It is worth reading as the de-facto regional norm for supplement/beauty chat selling.

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — "13 Skrip Step By Step Close Sale Di Whatsapp", https://anyflip.com/sctj/sfnm/basic

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Greeting; ask prospect's name and location (ice-breaker / rapport) |
| 2 | Respond to their location, find common ground; **first filtering question** (health status — pregnant, postpartum) |
| 3 | Product storytelling: suitability, ingredients, expected effect, how it works, *first* testimonials |
| 4 | **Attach 3 testimonial images (1st round)** ... later **add 2 more testimonial images (2nd round)** |
| 5 | **Second filtering question** — ask their specific problem |
| 6 | Match their stated problem to a relevant success story |
| 7 | Extra product info; suggest "try first" |
| 8 | **FIRST PRICE MENTION** — conversationally, immediately followed by 2-bottle upsell showing the saving |
| 9 | Upsell justification (why 2 units is better value) |
| 10–11 | Ask for delivery address |
| 12 | Ask which bank they prefer |
| 13 | Confirm amount, give account details, thank them |

Key structural facts: **images arrive at step 4 of 13 — before price at step 8.** Testimonial images are deliberately **drip-fed in two batches (3, then 2)**, not dumped.

A separate 50-tip Malay WhatsApp sales guide independently confirms the same drip pattern:

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — "50 Tips Close Sale WhatsApp", https://pdfcoffee.com/50-tips-close-sale-whatsapppdf-pdf-free.html
- Tip 46: *"Send 2–3 testimonial images, discuss benefits, wait, then send 2–3 more"* — explicitly to avoid overwhelming the prospect. It also says to include **two different kinds** of testimonial: (a) product-effectiveness testimonials and (b) **shipping/delivery-reliability testimonials** (proof that parcels actually arrive).

That second category is a distinctly regional insight: buyers are hedging against *seller* risk, not only *product* risk.

### 1b. Photo reviews / real photos as the trust unit

**[CITED] (vendor, but consistent with marketplace behaviour)** — Malaysian shoppers specifically seek **buyer-uploaded photos** because "photo reviews are harder to fake and give a realistic view of the actual product"; sellers are advised to chase photo reviews post-delivery. https://www.bigseller.com/blog/articleDetails/3479/product-listing-e-commerce-malaysia.htm

**[INFERENCE]** The Malay phrase "gambar sebenar" (actual/real photo) is a standing buyer demand in MY social commerce — i.e. a studio render is *not* proof; a phone photo of the real product on a real table is. I could not find a study quantifying this, so treat as inference. **[NO DATA FOUND]** on relative conversion of studio vs. phone photography in SEA chat.

### 1c. Generic image-conversion data (NOT chat-specific)

The only reasonably-sourced image data is web-storefront, not chat:

**[CITED] (industry research)** — Salsify 2025 Consumer Research Report: **77% of shoppers say high-quality images and video are important to their purchase decision**; 71% of consumers have returned a product because it didn't match the description. https://www.salsify.com/blog/what-types-of-product-imagery-earn-ecommerce-sales

**[CITED] (vendor, unverified)** — commonly recycled figures: "94% higher conversion for high-quality vs low-quality photos"; "60% of shoppers want 3–4 images". https://letsenhance.io/blog/all/product-image-quality/ — **I could not trace these to a primary study. Do not quote them to a customer.**

**[NO DATA FOUND]** — I found **no** credible study measuring conversion lift from sending images *inside a chat conversation* in MY/SG/BN/HK. Vendor blogs assert 5–15% WhatsApp conversion vs 1–4% web (e.g. https://botcake.io/blog/whatsapp-sales-converting-conversations-into-revenue), and one HK vendor claims chat commerce converts "1000%+ higher" for Sa Sa (https://blog.omnichat.ai/sasa-success-story/). These are self-reported vendor case studies with no methodology. **The "3–4 images" number is the only image-quantity figure with a named source, and it is US web data.**

### 1d. CRITICAL REGULATORY CONSTRAINT ON VISUALS — this overrides "send before/after"

Before/after imagery is the intuitive answer and it is **legally dangerous in this exact category**.

**[CITED] (regulator, Malaysia)** — Malaysia's Medicine Advertisements Board (MAB) guideline: ads "should not contain improper, exaggerated or misleading claims **or visuals to represent changes in the human body**". Testimonials are permitted but the person must genuinely exist and be accountable, **written consent (name, IC, signature, contact) is required**, and every testimonial ad must carry the line *"The effect of the product may vary among individuals."* The words "complete cure" and "guaranteed" are banned; 20 diseases (incl. cancer, diabetes, heart disease) cannot be referenced at all. Summarised at https://www.lamanify.com/guide/kkliu-guideline ; primary guideline: https://pharmacy.moh.gov.my/sites/default/files/document-upload/latest-guideline-advertising-medicines-and-medicinal-products-general-public.pdf (this PDF was unreachable at time of research — refused connection — so the clause wording above is from the secondary summary, flagged accordingly).

**[CITED] (regulator, Malaysia)** — MAB/KKLIU approval is required **before** any dissemination of product/health information, and "all types of dissemination of information about products and health services **including on the internet** are classified as advertisements." The KKLIU number, plus advertiser name, address and contact number, must be displayed on the advertisement. https://pharmacy.moh.gov.my/en/faq/how-ensure-advertisement-has-been-approved-mab.html

**[CITED] (regulator, Singapore)** — Health supplements in SG must not be "labelled, advertised or promoted for any specific medicinal purpose", including any suggestion of treating/preventing disease. HSA notes brands "frequently rely on before-and-after testimonials that reference medical conditions, which can be interpreted as disease treatment claims and therefore prohibited." https://www.hsa.gov.sg/announcements/health-supplement-regulations/ and https://www.hsa.gov.sg/therapeutic-products/advertisements

**[CITED] (regulator, Singapore)** — For healthcare *services* (clinics/aesthetics), before-and-after images are outright banned from websites, social media and paid ads under the Healthcare Services (Advertisement) Regulations 2021 (in force 3 Jan 2022) — permitted only in-premises during consultation. Note: this regulation covers licensed healthcare services, **not** supplement retail. Do not conflate. https://www.thehealthcollective.sg/articles/phmc-hcsa-advertising-regulations

**[INFERENCE]** Net effect for the bot: the visual that is *both* persuasive and safest is **the real product, in real hands, with real packaging and the registration number visible** — plus **order/shipping proof** — rather than body-transformation imagery. Before/after body images are the highest-legal-risk, highest-scam-signal asset in the deck for MY and SG.

### 1e. Recommended visual order

**[INFERENCE, built on 1a + 1d]** — no source gives an explicit ordering, so this is my synthesis of the two practitioner scripts plus the regulatory constraints:

1. **Real product photo** (actual unit, packaging legible) — early, as soon as product is named.
2. **Label / registration close-up** (MAL number, halal mark) — when the buyer shows any hesitation, or unprompted in MY/BN.
3. **2–3 testimonial screenshots** — before price. Text testimonials/chat screenshots, not body photos.
4. **Price card image sent in the same message as the price text.**
5. **Shipping/order proof** (packed parcels, tracking screenshots, "orders sent today") — at the objection/hesitation stage.
6. **2–3 more testimonials** — only if still hesitating (the "2nd round" from step 4 of the 13-step script).
7. Unboxing / demo video — optional, on request. **[NO DATA FOUND]** on whether video outperforms images in chat in these markets.

## 2. TRUST SIGNALS BY MARKET — and what kills trust fastest

### Malaysia

**[CITED] (regulator/news)** — Malaysian online sellers are **legally required** to display, before purchase: full price including taxes and delivery; business operator name; **business/company registration number**; contact info (email/phone/address); description of main characteristics; payment methods; T&Cs; expected delivery time. Penalty up to **RM50,000 fine and/or 3 years' jail**. The "PM for price / PM for details" tactic is explicitly illegal. https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/pm-for-details-as-a-selling-tactic-is-illegal.html and https://www.malaymail.com/news/tech-gadgets/2019/03/25/pm-for-details-as-a-selling-tactic-is-illegal/1736238

**[CITED] (legal)** — The instrument is the Consumer Protection (Electronic Trade Transactions) Regulations 2012, **revoked and replaced by the 2024 Regulations, in force 25 Dec 2024**, with broadly similar disclosure duties. https://www.kiizen.com.my/consumer-protection-electronic-trade-transaction-regulations-2024/

> **This is the single most important finding in this document.** The bot's current failure mode — withholding price to force engagement — is not merely bad practice in Malaysia, it is the exact tactic the regulator named and criminalised, and Malaysian buyers know it. See Rule 12.

**[CITED] (regulator)** — Health supplements sold in MY are expected to carry an NPRA-registered **MAL number**; a MAL number confirms the ingredient list matches the label and the labelling complies. It does **not** certify efficacy. https://emerhub.com/malaysia/product-registration-in-malaysia/

**[CITED] (regulator/industry)** — Halal certification in Malaysia is **voluntary in general**, but becomes **mandatory if the product is labelled or marketed as halal**. Product must be NPRA-registered *before* it can apply to JAKIM for halal. Halal certification does not substitute for product registration. https://label.pin-global.com/en/blog/malaysia-halal-pharmaceutical

**[CITED] (regulator/industry)** — If the seller is an MLM/direct-selling company, MY law requires an **AJL direct-selling licence** from KPDN (needs Sdn Bhd + RM500,000 paid-up capital + DSAM membership); KPDN publishes a public list of licensed companies, searchable **by registered legal entity name, not brand name**. Recruitment-led income = illegal pyramid scheme. https://checkmlm.com/guides/how-to-check-kpdn-license

**[CITED] (bank/police)** — MY buyers are actively taught to **check the seller's bank account number against the police "Semak Mule" portal** (ccid.rmp.gov.my/semakmule) before transferring. Named red flags: **bank transfer as the only payment option**, and **no physical address or customer support details**. https://www.cimb.com.my/en/personal/help-support/security-and-fraud/security-and-fraud-awareness/online-shopping-scams.html , https://says.com/my/tech/pdrm-scammer-online-shopping

**[CITED] (industry/news)** — COD persists as a trust mechanism: ~14% of MY e-commerce payments (2024); Statista 2024 cited at 34% of MY buyers preferring COD for non-essential/impulse purchases; 63.5% of Malaysians outside Klang Valley prefer cash as "more secure". https://www.pos.com.my/knowledge-hub/malaysians-prefer-cash-on-delivery-cod , https://parceldaily.com/blog/cash-on-delivery-how-it-works/ *(the 34%/63.5% figures are second-hand citations of Statista in a logistics blog — medium confidence)*

### Singapore

**[CITED] (regulator)** — **HSA does not pre-approve health supplements.** Therefore *"HSA-approved"* is itself a false claim HSA names as misleading, alongside "100% safe", promises of rapid results, "clinically proven" efficacy, and disease-prevention claims. Products may appear on a **voluntary notified list**. Buyers are told to buy from sellers with an **established retail presence in Singapore** and to treat rapid-result claims as a red flag for undeclared potent ingredients. https://www.hsa.gov.sg/announcements/health-supplement-regulations/

**[CITED] (news/legal)** — Supplying adulterated products: up to **3 years' jail and/or SGD 100,000** fine; HSA removes listings and warns sellers found selling adulterated supplements on local e-commerce/social platforms. https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=7e4bf72a-15ba-4145-a6b5-426bbec02d8a , https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/measures-to-ensure-suppliers'-compliance-with-regulations-for-health-supplements

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — SG practitioners say the checkout must offer **PayNow**, keep checkout native, and "show trust signals at the point of sale"; click-to-WhatsApp leads cost SGD 1.50–5. https://mediaplus.com.sg/social-commerce-in-singapore/

### Brunei

**[CITED] (regulator)** — Halal is materially stricter than MY: under the **Halal Certificate and Label Order (Amendment) 2017**, all businesses in Brunei producing/supplying/serving food and beverages must obtain a Halal Permit (Label) or Halal Certificate. Halal medicinal products, traditional medicines and health supplements may not contain components from non-halal animals or animals not slaughtered per Islamic law, and must be free of najis. Importers need **Ministry of Health import permits for all health supplement imports**. https://halaltreatments.com/2014/07/15/brunei-darussalam-guidelines-for-halal-certification/ , https://carraglobe.com/importer-of-record-brunei/ , https://www.gov.bn/Lists/Service/NewDisplay.aspx?ID=145

**[INFERENCE]** In BN, halal proof is closer to a gate than a bonus: absence of a visible halal mark is likely disqualifying rather than merely weakening. I found **[NO DATA FOUND]** on Bruneian consumer survey data specifically about chat sellers.

### Hong Kong

**[CITED] (news/consumer council)** — HK buyers of beauty/wellness are primed to expect hard-sell abuse. **Beauty and fitness account for nearly 90% of the sectors most prone to improper selling practices**, and >5,000 prepaid-service complaints over six years (about half of all such disputes), average case value **>HKD 30,000**, some over a million. The Consumer Council named and reprimanded a beauty group for high-pressure sales; tactics cited include "harassment, coercion and sweet talk". The government has now proposed a statutory **7-day cooling-off period and 14-day refund window** for prepaid beauty and fitness contracts. https://www.consumer.org.hk/en/press-release/pretty-beauty , https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/society/article/3358769/hong-kong-proposes-cooling-period-beauty-gym-deals-curb-hard-sell-tactics , https://www.consumer.org.hk/en/advocacy/study-report/cooling-off

**[CITED] (news/bank)** — WhatsApp is the most actively exploited scam channel in HK (est. ~90% smartphone-population usage), with impersonation the top scam type; the police Anti-Deception Coordination Centre hotline **18222** is widely publicised. https://cybersecuritycampaign.com.hk/phishing-scams/whatsapp-scams-hong-kong , https://www.hsbc.com.hk/help/cybersecurity-and-fraud/whatsapp-scams/

**[INFERENCE]** HK is the market where a hard close is most likely to be *actively pattern-matched to a known abuse* rather than merely disliked. Offering a stated refund/cooling-off policy unprompted is disproportionately valuable in HK.

### What kills trust fastest — ranked

**[CITED]** items 1–3, **[INFERENCE]** for the ranking itself:
1. Refusing to give the price when asked (MY: illegal and widely known to be; source above).
2. Bank transfer to a **personal** account as the only option, with no business name / registration number / address (named red flag by CIMB & PDRM).
3. Miracle-cure or rapid-result claims (named as misleading by both HSA and MAB).
4. **[INFERENCE]** Studio-only imagery with no real-product photo, and testimonials that look screenshot-farmed.
5. **[INFERENCE]** Urgency stacking ("last 2 units", "promo ends tonight") repeated across days — self-invalidating and, in HK, matches the Consumer Council's named coercion pattern.

## 3. PACING — how many exchanges before price

### What the evidence actually supports

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — The 13-step MY script quotes price at **step 8 of 13**, after: rapport, two filtering questions, product story, and 3 testimonial images. https://anyflip.com/sctj/sfnm/basic

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — A widely-circulated MY 4-step framework opens with the explicit rule **"Don't give price first"**, with the deflection script: *"Maaf sis sebelum itu boleh saya bertanyakan beberapa soalan, sebab saya nak pastikan adakah produk ini sesuai atau tidak kepada sis"* ("Sorry sis, before that may I ask a few questions, because I want to make sure whether this product suits you"). Then rapport ("Instant Rapport"), then the problem question *"Sis nak beli produk ini sebab masalah apa yer? Mana lah tahu saya boleh bantu?"*, then close with quantity + bundle discount: *"Berapa unit sis nak cuba? Kalau beli 2 unit akan dapat diskaun 20%"*. https://fames.my/4-langkah-mudah-close-sale-guna-whatsapp/

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — A third MY source names the canonical sequence: **Break The Brick → Asking → Sharing (testimonies) → Explaining → Pitching (price) → Upselling → Close The Deal**, and lists as explicit mistakes: "giving price immediately without introducing the product", "being too pushy", "failing to follow up". https://bumbu.agency/cara-mudah-close-sales-tips-close-sales-dengan-cepat/

So the **practitioner consensus in Malaysia is unambiguous: price comes after problem-discovery and after social proof — roughly the 5th–8th turn.**

### The counterweight — and it is strong

**[CITED] (practitioner, same corpus)** — The 50-tips guide is candid about the trade-off: **Tip 22 — not displaying price yields more leads but lower quality; displaying price yields fewer but qualified leads.** And **Tip 48 — busy customers hate lengthy questioning; skip unnecessary detail and ask "Need product info, or proceed?"** https://pdfcoffee.com/50-tips-close-sale-whatsapppdf-pdf-free.html

**[CITED] (regulator)** — And in Malaysia the law is on the buyer's side of this argument (Section 2).

**[INFERENCE]** The resolution: pacing should be **buyer-controlled, not seller-controlled**. Slow discovery is the default because it demonstrably raises order value in-market; but a direct price question is a legitimate instruction that must be obeyed — at the *latest* on second asking, and in MY on first asking. The correct move is never to refuse the price; it is to *answer the price and immediately add the framing* (see Section 4), so the number never arrives naked.

### Response speed

**[CITED] (vendor, widely-recycled)** — Velocify-attributed figures: contacting a lead within one minute lifts conversion **391%**; waiting >5 minutes cuts qualification odds by 80%; companies responding within 5 min are 21× more likely to qualify vs 30 min; 78% of customers buy from the first responder. https://www.teamgate.com/blog/lead-response-time-study-speed-impacts-revenue/ , https://caseyresponse.com/blog/lead-response-time-statistics — **B2B web-lead data, not SEA chat commerce; the primary Velocify/InsideSales studies are old and hard to verify. Directionally safe, numerically unreliable.**

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — MY sources independently say the same in local terms: reply as fast as possible, and if you were slow, **apologise** so the prospect reads you as polite ("sopan"). https://bumbu.agency/cara-mudah-close-sales-tips-close-sales-dengan-cepat/

### Follow-up cadence

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — Two named cadences in circulation in MY:
- **3-7-30**: follow up on day 3, day 7, day 30 — described as keeping the relationship warm "without making them feel forced". https://bumbu.agency/cara-mudah-close-sales-tips-close-sales-dengan-cepat/
- **Day 1 / Day 3 / Day 7**: D1 "have you seen the product?", D3 "promo last day", D7 "new stock in". https://profsiber.com/penat-ambil-order-dan-close-sale-di-whatsapp/

**[NO DATA FOUND]** — no controlled test of follow-up cadence in these markets.

## 4. PRICE PRESENTATION — making the number not feel transactional

All of the following are attested in-market practitioner behaviour:

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — **Three-tier pricing.** Tip 37: use three price tiers; most buyers choose the middle, perceiving it as better value. https://pdfcoffee.com/50-tips-close-sale-whatsapppdf-pdf-free.html

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — **Anchored discount with deadline for the "wait for payday" objection.** Tip 35: *"Original RM__ → Now RM__. Promotion ends soon."*

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — **Never cut price; add value instead.** Tips 33–34: when asked for a discount, respond with a **free gift (state its RM value)**, **free shipping**, or a **money-back guarantee** — not a margin cut. *(Caution: see Section 2 — the word "guaranteed" is on Malaysia's MAB banned-terms list for product claims. A shipping/satisfaction guarantee is a different thing from a health-outcome guarantee, but the bot must never say "guaranteed results".)*

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — **Bundle laddering at the moment of price.** The 13-step script pairs the first price with an immediate 2-bottle upsell showing the saving (step 8→9); the 4-step framework closes with "2 units = 20% discount". https://anyflip.com/sctj/sfnm/basic , https://fames.my/4-langkah-mudah-close-sale-guna-whatsapp/

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — **Avoid transactional vocabulary.** Tip 11: avoid *"beli"* (buy) and *"bayar"* (pay); use *"try"*, *"suggest"*, *"order"*, *"pick"*.

### Cost-per-day framing

**[CITED] (vendor, weak)** — the "less than a cup of coffee" / divide-by-365 reframe is standard sales practice; a Rain Group figure of "works in 72% of price discussions" is quoted at https://www.mypminterview.com/p/mastering-price-framing-strategies — **I could not verify this against Rain Group's own publication. Treat the 72% as unusable.** The technique itself is real and universal. **[NO DATA FOUND]** on its effectiveness specifically in MY/SG/BN/HK wellness chat.

**[INFERENCE]** For an RM188–RM1180 ladder, cost-per-day is the single most useful reframe available (RM1180 over a 90-day program ≈ RM13/day), and it is compatible with the regional habit of quoting the *promo price against a struck-through retail price*.

### Instalments / BNPL

**[CITED] (vendor/official)** — **Atome** in Malaysia: 3 interest-free payments at 17,000+ merchants, positioned as **Shariah-compliant** (i-Cash financing under Tawarruq), plus 3/6/12-month options on the Atome Visa card. https://www.atome.my/en-my — Shariah-compliance is a meaningful trust signal for the Muslim-majority MY/BN segment. **[NO DATA FOUND]** on BNPL attach-rate for wellness specifically.

### Free-shipping threshold

**[CITED] (vendor, methodology stated)** — Standard formula: set the free-shipping threshold **15–30% above current AOV** (e.g. RM120 AOV → RM138–156); attributed to Shopify merchant data and testing across MY/SG brands. Real MY examples: BookXcess free shipping at RM80 nett; Shopee's RM40 minimum. https://webmedic.com/ecommerce-free-shipping-strategy , https://parceldaily.com/blog/guide-to-e-commerce-shipping-in-malaysia/

### Payment rails by market — practical trust detail

**[CITED]**
- **MY:** DuitNow QR (national interoperable QR), DuitNow Transfer, FPX; COD still relevant. https://curlec.com/blog/guides/fpx-vs-duitnow/
- **SG:** PayNow expected at checkout. https://mediaplus.com.sg/social-commerce-in-singapore/
- **HK:** credit cards ~40%, AlipayHK ~15%, PayMe ~15%, Octopus ~10%, FPS ~10%, PayPal ~10%. Small merchants often prefer **PayMe** (lower fees, instant settlement) and share a **PayMe QR or paycode URL over WhatsApp**. https://www.transfi.com/blog/popular-local-payment-methods-and-solutions-in-hong-kong , https://statrys.com/blog/hk-payment-methods
- **MY↔SG:** DuitNow–PayNow real-time cross-border link is live. https://wise.com/sg/blog/paynow-to-duitnow-guide
- **BN:** **[NO DATA FOUND]** — I could not find reliable data on BIBD QuickPay / Brunei chat-seller payment norms.

## 5. LINKS IN CHAT

### The practitioner/vendor position

**[CITED] (vendor)** — "The most effective WhatsApp conversational commerce framework has **no external links**"; the recommendation is in-chat catalogues and in-chat confirm/pay so the buyer never switches apps. https://pickyassist.com/blog/whatsapp-conversational-commerce-in-conversion/ , https://www.flowcart.ai/blog/whatsapp-commerce — vendor-interested (they sell in-chat checkout), so treat the *causal* claim as unproven; the *behavioural* claim (app-switching is friction) is uncontroversial.

**[CITED] (vendor)** — Counter-nuance worth keeping: "instead of sending a generic product link, pairing it with a quick voice note or text message explaining **why** you're recommending it ... yields higher click-through rates." https://gmcsco.com/how-to-sell-on-whatsapp/

### WhatsApp-specific caveats

**[CITED] (Meta platform policy + vendors)** — WhatsApp spam enforcement: accounts are flagged/banned on block-and-report rates; **shortened links that hide the destination (bit.ly etc.) are treated as a potential security threat**; keep to roughly 1–4 messages per hour per active chat; message only users who opted in / messaged first; don't blast identical messages. https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/policy-enforcement , https://support.wati.io/en/articles/11463217-why-your-whatsapp-business-account-waba-may-get-banned-or-restricted-and-how-to-avoid-it , https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-business-banned

**[NO DATA FOUND]** — nothing credible on whether WhatsApp *link previews* specifically raise or lower conversion.

### Instagram-specific caveats — stronger and more concrete

**[CITED] (vendor, but specific and consistent across sources)** — "Links in cold DMs are one of the strongest spam signals Instagram uses. **No links in your first message, no links in your follow-ups. Wait until someone replies before sharing anything clickable.**" Inappropriate link-sending is described as getting a profile silently flagged "low trust", killing Explore reach. Mitigation: never send a bare link — always one line of context first. https://www.inro.social/blog/link-in-dm , https://www.usewave.co/blog/instagram-dm-limits , https://www.ytviews.in/instagram-dm-link-restrictions/

**[INFERENCE]** Practical synthesis: **links are a late-stage, low-frequency, high-context tool.** Zero links before the buyer has replied at least twice; never a shortener; never a bare URL; never more than one link per conversation stage; prefer sending an *image of the price card* over a link to a price page. Google Drive catalogue links are the worst of all worlds (unfamiliar domain, permissions friction, looks like a phishing pattern) — this last point is inference, not data.

## 6. VOICE / LANGUAGE NORMS BY MARKET

### Malaysia

**[CITED] (practitioner)** — Use **"bahasa pasar"** (market language) — the everyday spoken/WhatsApp register — for beauty and health products; short sentences but still clear. https://profsiber.com/penat-ambil-order-dan-close-sale-di-whatsapp/

### What the MLM model implies for the bot

**[INFERENCE]** The consistent structural feature across Herbalife, Nu Skin and Shaklee is that **the offer is not a product, it is an assessment plus a program plus a coach**. The RM188–RM1180 ladder maps naturally onto that: a program duration (e.g. 30/60/90 days), not three SKUs. Two concrete borrowings:
1. **The evaluation as the opening ask.** Herbalife's body-composition evaluation and the 13-step script's two "filtering questions" are the same device: earn the right to prescribe. This is the honest, non-pushy substitute for asking for the sale early.
2. **The 12-week program frame** justifies both the ticket size and the cost-per-day reframe, and it converts a one-off transaction into a stated commitment with check-ins — which is also what makes follow-up messages welcome rather than nagging.

**Compliance caution [CITED]:** Herbalife's own Rules of the Road and MY's MAB rules both constrain what a distributor may claim; and MY MLM operators need a valid **AJL licence** (Section 2). A bot speaking on behalf of a direct-selling brand inherits those constraints. It must never promise weight/health outcomes.

## CONCRETE RULES FOR THE BOT

Numbered, implementable. Each rule notes whether its basis is **[C]** cited or **[I]** inference.

### A. Visual proof

1. **Never mention a price without an image attached to the same message.** The first price message must carry a real product photo or a price card. **[I]** (built on the 13-step script placing images before price, **[C]**)
2. **Send the first real product photo by the third bot message, regardless of pacing.** It must be an actual-unit photo with legible packaging — not a render, not a lifestyle stock image. **[I]**
3. **Drip testimonials in batches of 2–3, never more, with talk between batches.** First batch before price; second batch only if the buyer hesitates after price. **[C]** (Tip 46; 13-step script step 4)
4. **Include one delivery-proof asset per conversation** (packed parcel / tracking screenshot / "sent out today") at the hesitation stage. Buyers are hedging seller risk, not just product risk. **[C]** (Tip 46 explicitly names shipping testimonials)
5. **Send a label/registration close-up** (MAL number, halal mark, batch/expiry) whenever authenticity, safety or halal comes up — and unprompted in MY and BN. **[C]** for the signals' importance; **[I]** for the trigger timing
6. **Never send before/after body-transformation images.** Hard ban. MY MAB prohibits visuals representing changes in the human body; SG HSA treats before/after testimonials referencing conditions as prohibited disease-treatment claims. **[C]**
7. **Cap at 6 images per conversation and never send two image messages back-to-back without buyer text in between.** **[I]** (anti-spam, anti-bot-tell; consistent with the drip rule)
8. **If a testimonial is shown, attach the disclaimer "Results may vary between individuals"** (MY MAB requires "The effect of the product may vary among individuals"). **[C]**

### B. Price and pacing

9. **Default: do not quote price before the buyer's problem has been stated and acknowledged** — practically, exchange 4–5. **[C]** (three independent MY practitioner sources)
10. **Hard override: if the buyer asks for price, give the price.** Give it on the **first** ask in Malaysia; give it on the **second** ask at the latest everywhere else. **[C]** (MY law + Tip 48)
11. **One deflection maximum, and only ever as a question the buyer can decline.** Permitted form: *"Boleh saya tanya 2 soalan dulu so I can quote you the right pack? Or I can just send the full price list now — up to you."* Then obey the answer. **[I]**
12. **Never use "PM for price", "DM me for details", or any equivalent, in any market.** In Malaysia this is the specific tactic the ministry declared illegal (up to RM50,000 / 3 years). **[C]**
13. **Every price message must contain: the number, the unit, what's included, and the delivery cost** — not the number alone. MY law requires full price including taxes and transport. **[C]**
14. **Never quote a bare single price.** Always present the ladder: struck-through retail → member/promo price → bundle. Three tiers, with the intended sale in the middle. **[C]** (Tips 35, 37)
15. **Attach a cost-per-day reframe to any price above RM400**, tied to the program duration (e.g. "RM1,180 for the 90-day program ≈ RM13/day"). **[I]** (technique is **[C]** generically; regional efficacy unproven)
16. **On "mahal" / "too expensive": never reduce the price.** Respond with a named-value free gift, free shipping, or a longer-payment option. **[C]** (Tips 33–34)
17. **Offer BNPL/instalments explicitly for the top tier in MY/SG** (Atome — note it is marketed as Shariah-compliant, which matters for MY/BN). **[C]**
18. **State a free-shipping threshold set 15–30% above current AOV**, and surface it at the moment of the bundle offer. **[C]**
19. **Name the payment rail the buyer expects: MY → DuitNow QR / FPX / COD; SG → PayNow; HK → PayMe or FPS.** Do not default to "bank transfer to my account". **[C]**
20. **Vocabulary: avoid "beli"/"bayar"/"buy"/"pay" in the pitch.** Use "try", "start with", "order", "pick". **[C]** (Tip 11)
21. **One ask per message. Never stack two questions.** **[I]**
22. **Ask for the sale exactly once per conversation, and only after price + at least one objection has been handled.** On a "no", stop pitching and offer to send info for later. **[C]** for "don't pressure"; **[I]** for the once-only rule

### C. Trust signals

23. **Include the legal business name and company registration number in the conversation before payment**, plus a contactable phone/address. This is a statutory disclosure in MY and a top-ranked anti-scam signal. **[C]**
24. **Never say "HSA-approved", "HSA-registered", "100% safe", "clinically proven", "guaranteed results", "complete cure", or "cures/treats/prevents [disease]".** HSA does not approve supplements; MY bans "complete cure"/"guaranteed" and 20 named diseases. Maintain this as a hard blocklist with per-market extensions. **[C]**
25. **Permitted claim shape only: "supports X", "helps maintain Y"** — normal-function language. **[C]** (HSA)
26. **MY/BN: lead with halal status and be precise about it.** If halal-certified, name the certifying body; if it is not certified, never imply it is — in MY, marketing a product as halal without valid certification is itself the offence. In BN, treat halal proof as a gate. **[C]**
27. **MY: cite the NPRA MAL number when asked about safety, and never claim it means the product works.** **[C]**
28. **If the brand is a direct-selling company in MY, be able to state the AJL licence holder's registered Sdn Bhd name** (KPDN's list is searchable by legal entity, not brand). **[C]**
29. **HK: proactively state the refund / cooling-off policy before asking for payment.** Beauty and fitness are ~90% of HK's improper-selling complaints and a statutory 7-day cooling-off + 14-day refund window has been proposed. **[C]**
30. **Offer COD or an escrowed/marketplace option where available in MY** rather than requiring an upfront transfer to an unknown account. **[C]**
31. **If the buyer says "scam" or asks "are you legit"**, respond with a fixed verification bundle: legal name + registration number + physical address + product registration number + an invitation to verify independently. Do not respond emotionally or with more testimonials. **[I]**

### D. Links

32. **No links before the buyer has sent at least two messages.** On Instagram, no link in the first message or any follow-up until they reply — links in cold DMs are among Instagram's strongest spam signals. **[C]**
33. **Never use a URL shortener.** WhatsApp treats destination-hiding links as a security threat. **[C]**
34. **Never send a bare URL.** One line of context first, then the link, then stop. **[C]**
35. **Maximum one link per conversation stage, and prefer an image over a link** for anything the buyer only needs to *look at* (price list, ingredients, certificate). **[I]**
36. **Do not send Google Drive / Dropbox catalogue links.** Unfamiliar domain + permission friction + phishing resemblance. **[I]**
37. **Never send a link in place of an answer.** Answer in chat first; the link is supplementary. **[C]** (in-chat-completion consensus; vendor-sourced)

### E. Voice, language, anti-bot

38. **Mirror the buyer's language and register, including code-switching** — if they write Manglish, write Manglish; if BM, BM; if traditional Chinese, traditional Chinese. Mirror their address term ("sis", "dear", "boss") rather than inventing one. **[C]** (Tip 1)
39. **Scale formality up with ticket size**, and default to a *more restrained* register in SG and HK than in MY. **[C]** for the price/formality link; **[I]** for the market ranking
40. **Message length: 2–3 lines per message maximum.** No walls of text. **[C]** (Tips 30–32)
41. **No triple punctuation ("!!!", "???"); it reads as anger.** Emojis are fine and expected in MY, sparse in SG/HK. **[C]** for the punctuation rule; **[I]** for the per-market emoji density
42. **Never criticise a competitor product the buyer has used.** Validate, then match a testimonial to their case. **[C]**
43. **Vary openers.** No two conversations should begin with the identical sentence. **[I]**
44. **Always respond to the buyer's actual last message before advancing the script.** Script-step advancement that ignores buyer input is the loudest bot-tell. **[I]**
45. **Do not reply in under ~10 seconds, and respect published business hours** — acknowledge off-hours explicitly rather than answering instantly at 3am as if staffed. **[I]** (MAE Global's published window is Mon–Fri 9–5, **[C]**)
46. **Reply fast within hours, though — target under 5 minutes in-hours**, and apologise for a slow reply if one occurs. **[C]** (speed data is weak/B2B; the apology norm is MY practitioner-attested)
47. **Do not claim to be human, and do not volunteer being a bot unprompted; if asked directly, answer truthfully and offer a human handover.** Discovering the deception is the documented trust-killer. **[C]**
48. **Provide a human-handover path for anything sensitive** — medical conditions, pregnancy, medication interactions, refunds, complaints. **[C]** (HK vendor guidance; also implied by MY/SG claim rules)
49. **Never re-send an asset already sent in the same conversation.** **[I]**
50. **Follow-up cadence: at most day 1, day 3, day 7, then day 30 — and stop permanently on any "no" or non-reply after the day-7 touch.** Each follow-up must carry new information, not a repeat of the ask. **[C]** (3-7-30 and D1/D3/D7 are both attested; the "stop" rule is **[I]**)
51. **Never repeat a scarcity or deadline claim that has already passed.** If "promo ends tonight" was said on Monday, it cannot be said again on Thursday. **[I]**
52. **Frame the offer as an assessment → program → coach, not a bundle of SKUs.** Ask the two filtering questions (suitability/health status, then the specific problem) before recommending, mirroring both the MY 13-step script and the Herbalife/Nu Skin coaching model. **[C]** for both structures; **[I]** for the recommendation to merge them

### F. Per-market switches to implement

53. **MY:** price on first ask; halal + MAL number forward; DuitNow/COD; Manglish/BM permitted; 3-7-30 follow-up; must be able to state Sdn Bhd name + registration number. **[C]**
54. **SG:** claim blocklist at its strictest ("HSA-approved" is itself a false claim); PayNow; restrained register; no "sis/dear"; no urgency stacking. **[C]**
55. **BN:** halal is a gate not a bonus; Malay-dominant; conservative imagery; MoH import-permit context. **[C]** for the halal/permit facts; **[I]** for register and imagery
56. **HK:** fast and short but zero pressure; state refund/cooling-off unprompted; PayMe/FPS; traditional Chinese; be aware buyers are primed by documented beauty-sector hard-sell abuse and by WhatsApp scam prevalence. **[C]**
